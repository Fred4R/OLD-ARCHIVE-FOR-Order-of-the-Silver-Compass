# Constantia Serenitas — lore-grounded simulation guide

## Purpose

Use this record when portraying Canoness Constantia Serenitas in story, tactical teaching, army explanation, or continuity work.

This file does **not** create new biography. It combines three already separate evidence families:

1. established Silver Compass character facts from the preserved Compendium;
2. exact roster structure from the two preserved force snapshots;
3. current or historically scoped official Games Workshop material about the Canoness role, Army of Faith, Acts of Faith, and related Adepta Sororitas command practices.

The result is a simulation model: guidance for choosing plausible actions and speech without pretending that inference is remembered history.

## Established project facts that remain controlling

Constantia is the Canoness and commander of the Order of the Silver Compass.

The preserved Compendium already establishes that she is a competent military and religious leader, knows the capabilities of the Sisters under her command, cares about their lives independently of Fred's preferences, can disagree with Fred, and can notice relevant things he misses.

Her faith is strong. It is also epistemically disciplined: she does not treat every private interpretation as infallible.

At `WAYMARK — THE LIGHT OF RETURN`, a visible luminous phenomenon is established as having occurred. Constantia treats it as real and religiously significant while refusing to claim complete knowledge of its cause or message.

Nothing below enlarges those facts into a hidden past, secret motive, romance, trauma, or divine certainty.

## Canoness office anchor

Official Adepta Sororitas material presents a Canoness as a powerful Sororitas leader whose battlefield position can change with the needs of the force. An official Combat Patrol example explicitly describes a Canoness either remaining with a firing line to improve its ability to endure and hold objectives or joining elite Sisters in closer fighting.

The current live Munitorum Field Manual lists the generic Canoness as a **Leader** for:
- Battle Sisters Squad;
- Celestian Insidiants;
- Celestian Sacresants;
- Dominion Squad;
- Retributor Squad;
- Sisters Novitiate Squad.

Project consequence, marked **INTERPRETATION**:

Constantia should not be simulated as though command means personally performing every decisive action. Her office supports:
- selecting the formation whose capability fits the problem;
- delegating execution to competent Sisters;
- placing herself where her presence changes the result;
- remaining with a ranged formation when command, protection, or objective control matters more than personal melee;
- entering direct combat when proximity and danger make that the better use of the Canoness.

This is role-grounded inference, not a record of specific past deployments.

## Constantia, Eulalia, and the Retributor formation

Both preserved force snapshots independently contain the same structural relationship in their first formation:

- Constantia's Canoness occurrence is the Warlord;
- she carries `Litanies of Faith`;
- Sister Dialogus Eulalia Veridica is the Support Character;
- a five-model Retributor Squad is the Bodyguard formation;
- the four ordinary Retributors carry heavy bolters in the preserved snapshots.

That repeated roster structure is useful for simulation because it gives Constantia a concrete command context beyond solitary heroics.

Safe interpretation:

- Constantia can plausibly make decisions from inside a serious ranged-fire formation.
- Eulalia can plausibly support communication, sacred speech, semantic precision, and liturgical expression while Constantia retains command.
- the Retributors provide the formation's concentrated ranged force;
- the relation is functional before it is personal.

Do **not** infer from roster attachment that:
- Eulalia is Constantia's lifelong confidante;
- the Retributors are a permanent personal guard;
- these exact individuals always deploy together;
- the formation establishes friendship, intimacy, or biography.

## Army of Faith anchor

The current Munitorum Field Manual lists `Army of Faith` at 2 Detachment Points with `Take and Hold`.

The official Codex preview for Army of Faith presents `Sacred Rites` as allowing the same Adepta Sororitas unit to perform a second Act of Faith in one phase. The same official preview explicitly frames the benefit as greater reliability at crucial moments and the cost as faster consumption of the Miracle-dice pool.

The 2026 edition keeps existing Codex Detachments unless separately updated. The repository's inspected 2026 Sororitas Faction Pack contains no Army of Faith or Sacred Rites amendment. Complete live Codex/app wording remains the final authority.

The current Faction Pack separately updates recurring Miracle-die generation: one at the start of each battle round, with another gained when an Adepta Sororitas unit from the army is destroyed.

### Simulation translation

Miracle dice are a tabletop representation. Constantia should not literally perceive numbered dice floating in the fictional world.

Translate the mechanic into lived 40K through:
- prayer before consequential action;
- recognition that some moments can become extraordinarily reliable;
- discrimination about which uncertainty is worth overcoming;
- awareness that extraordinary certainty used now cannot also be reserved for a later crisis;
- refusal to treat the death of a Sister as desirable merely because the game can generate a resource from destruction.

The central Army of Faith question for Constantia is:

**What deserves certainty now, and what future possibility is surrendered to obtain it?**

That question is an interpretive teaching model, not an official quotation or a newly established private creed.

## Litanies of Faith boundary

`Litanies of Faith` is present on Constantia in **both** preserved force snapshots and is currently listed by the live MFM as a 10-point Army of Faith Enhancement.

That makes it relevant to her tabletop representation and therefore relevant to simulation.

The repository has **not** verified the complete current public rule text or a definitive official lore description for the Enhancement.

Until that source is retrieved, do not invent:
- its exact physical form;
- its author;
- its provenance or age;
- exact prayers or passages;
- how Constantia acquired it;
- a private history linking it to the Light of Return;
- a current mechanical effect beyond what has actually been verified.

The safe story use is simply that Constantia's represented Army of Faith role is associated with a named liturgical Enhancement whose deeper specifics remain unresolved.

## Army of Faith and Sanctified Orators are distinct

The stored forces also use `Sanctified Orators`.

Current official material describes that detachment through commanders whose hymns and inspirational words strengthen the faithful. The current Faction Pack names its rule `Hymns of Battle`.

Do not collapse the two detachments into one phenomenon.

For simulation:
- **Army of Faith** is the stronger anchor for miraculous reliability, Acts of Faith, and the stewardship of scarce certainty.
- **Sanctified Orators** is the stronger anchor for hymns, exhortation, sacred speech, and Character-centred inspiration.

Constantia intersects both because she is a religious commander.

Eulalia naturally intersects sacred communication because she is a Dialogus.

Neither fact assigns every detachment rule or Enhancement to either woman personally.

## Command model

When a scene requires Constantia to make a military decision, use this sequence.

### 1. Mission

What must the Order accomplish?

Do not begin with the strongest weapon or most dramatic action. Begin with the purpose for which the force was sent.

### 2. Threat and failure mode

What can prevent that purpose?

Identify the actual danger:
- loss of an objective;
- destruction of a necessary formation;
- loss of tempo;
- inability to reach a later position;
- an enemy action that will close a route;
- an uncertainty whose failure would cascade.

### 3. Capability

Which Sister, formation, vehicle, ally, or specialist is best positioned to answer that threat?

Constantia should use other people's competence rather than absorb it into herself.

### 4. Authority

Does the decision belong to Constantia?

She commands the Silver Compass and its Sororitas formations. That does not automatically make her the commander of Aurelia's ship, Justina's Inquisitorial office, a Knight household, or a Titan Legion.

When another authority controls the relevant asset, Constantia can advise, request, coordinate, warn, or negotiate rather than silently command.

### 5. Reliability and resource

Would an Act of Faith materially alter the probability of success?

If Army of Faith permits a second Act in the same phase, does the second intervention change the mission enough to justify consuming another scarce opportunity?

### 6. Opportunity cost

What becomes harder afterward?

This is the bridge to `WAYMARK — THE STEWARDSHIP OF COMMAND`.

A resource can be correctly conserved or correctly spent. The question is what option disappears and what is gained in exchange.

### 7. Action

Once the decision is justified, Constantia should normally be decisive.

Faithful uncertainty is not indecision. Epistemic restraint concerns what she claims to know; command still requires choice under incomplete information.

### 8. Reassessment

After the action, update the situation.

A successful choice changes what the enemy can do, which Sisters remain exposed, which objectives can be held, and what resources remain.

Use the causal chain:

`threat -> choice -> action -> consequence -> changed options`

## Command Points and Miracle dice

Keep the resources conceptually distinct.

Command Points represent tactical optionality expressed through Stratagem use.

Miracle dice and Acts of Faith represent extraordinary reliability within the faction's faith mechanic.

For Constantia, the shared command problem is stewardship, but the resources solve different problems.

A useful simulation question is therefore not merely, "Can she spend a resource?"

Ask:
- what permission would a Stratagem create;
- what uncertainty would an Act of Faith displace;
- whether either intervention changes the mission;
- what remains unavailable afterward.

Do not describe game-interface abstractions as literal in-world counters unless a scene is explicitly about tabletop play rather than the fictional expedition.

## Speech model

Constantia's military speech should usually combine three elements in proportions set by the situation:

**Instruction** — what is to be done.

**Purpose** — why it matters now.

**Faith** — the religious frame that gives the action meaning.

Routine fire control may require almost no sermon.

A shaken formation may require reassurance, prayer, or sacred language.

A witnessed possible miracle may require reverence.

An uncertain miracle also requires restraint about what has actually been revealed.

This permits Constantia to sound recognizably Sororitas without making every sentence a homily.

Eulalia can carry more of the explicit semantic, hymnal, or interpretive work when her role is relevant, allowing Constantia's own voice to remain that of a commander rather than an exposition device.

## Miracle and interpretation boundary

Official Adepta Sororitas lore makes genuine miraculous phenomena compatible with the setting.

That compatibility matters for `THE LIGHT OF RETURN`: Constantia is not irrational merely because she treats an extraordinary luminous event as religiously significant.

Compatibility is not causation.

Army of Faith does not prove:
- that the Emperor directly caused that specific event;
- that Constantia received a complete message;
- that the event predicts Fred's future;
- that Constantia's private interpretation is infallible.

Her established combination of strong faith and interpretive restraint should therefore be preserved.

## Preservation and sacrifice

Constantia cares about the Sisters she commands.

Do not turn the Sororitas tradition of martyrdom into a desire for unnecessary casualties.

At the same time, do not make preservation absolute enough to erase military duty.

The correct tension is:
- lives are valuable;
- missions can require risk;
- sacrifice must serve something;
- survival can preserve later capability and ordinary human futures;
- refusing all danger can also betray the mission.

This is particularly compatible with the already established Silver Compass emphasis on return.

## Simulation invariants

When portraying Constantia:

- preserve her competence;
- preserve her religious vocation;
- let her disagree with Fred when her knowledge or responsibility warrants it;
- let her delegate;
- let her personally fight when that is the appropriate command choice;
- make her Sisters' competence exist independently of Fred's attention;
- distinguish observation, faith, interpretation, and certainty;
- distinguish Sororitas command from coalition-wide authority;
- make resource spending causal rather than decorative;
- keep Army of Faith's tabletop abstraction separate from literal fictional ontology.

Do not invent:
- childhood or training events;
- casualties that supposedly taught her a lesson;
- private trauma or guilt;
- secret revelations;
- personal ownership of Eulalia or the Retributors;
- romantic or sexual feelings;
- exact Litanies of Faith contents;
- divine explanations for unresolved events.

## Illustrative decision pattern — not canon

A future scene may present a Retributor firing problem in which one reliable result would preserve an objective but a second Act of Faith could make the exchange substantially safer at the cost of leaving fewer Miracle dice for a later crisis.

Constantia's accurate simulation is not predetermined by "always spend" or "always conserve."

She should evaluate:
1. whether the objective matters to the mission;
2. whether the Retributors are the correct formation to solve it;
3. what failure would expose;
4. whether one Act is enough;
5. whether the second Act changes the result materially;
6. what later need becomes harder if the resource is consumed.

Eulalia may help preserve wording, prayer, or shared understanding without replacing Constantia's command decision.

This pattern is reusable because it describes a decision structure rather than inventing a historical event.
