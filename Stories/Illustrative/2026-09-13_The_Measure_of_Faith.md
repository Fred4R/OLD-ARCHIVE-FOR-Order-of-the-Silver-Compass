# The Measure of Faith

The room remains aboard the True Meridian.

Aurelia's working table is still between you and the far side of the chamber. Constantia is nearer your left. Justina remains to your right. Eulalia is near the centre. The three Paragon pilots remain farther around the table. Two other women remain materially present at the farther edge without the record forcing names onto the unresolved count.

Aurelia clears most of the tactical display.

One thing remains.

A small pool of six-sided dice.

Constantia looks at them before she looks at the projected army.

"Miracle dice," Eulalia says.

Constantia gives a slight nod.

"Not miracles," she says. "The means by which the rules represent our Acts of Faith."

The distinction is deliberate.

The current instruction is simple enough to hear. The Order gains a Miracle die at the start of each battle round. Another enters the pool whenever an Adepta Sororitas unit from the army is destroyed.

Constantia does not call destruction desirable.

She does not call it payment.

She says, "Loss may leave something in the hands of those who remain. That does not make the loss good."

One of the Paragon pilots watches the dice.

"Army of Faith?"

"Two Detachment Points," Eulalia answers. "Take and Hold."

Constantia looks toward the battlefield projection.

Then she touches one die.

"Sacred Rites."

Army of Faith permits a Sororitas unit to perform a second Act of Faith in the same phase.

Constantia leaves the die where it is.

"The important word is not second."

She looks across the table.

"It is can."

Eulalia's attention shifts toward her.

Constantia continues.

"Permission is not obligation."

A second die is placed beside the first.

The choice becomes visible.

One Act of Faith may secure a crucial roll for which the army has a lawful substitution. Army of Faith can let the same unit call upon that certainty again in the same phase.

But the second die does not come from nowhere.

The pool becomes smaller.

A future choice disappears.

Constantia says, "Faith is not proved by emptying the reliquary because one is permitted to open it."

Aurelia's mouth moves slightly.

"Stewardship."

"Yes."

A steward is one entrusted with something that is not merely hers to waste.

One Paragon pilot asks, "Then why take Army of Faith?"

Constantia finally moves the second die.

"Because sometimes uncertainty is the thing that kills you."

She places it beside a projected Retributor position.

"Sometimes one assured result changes the exchange."

She places the first die.

"Sometimes two assured results change the battle."

The second follows.

"And sometimes spending both merely makes us feel decisive."

Justina speaks from your right.

"Those cases should not be confused."

Constantia glances toward her.

"No."

The table changes.

A narrow objective area appears.

Take and Hold.

Not yet the actual mission. A Force Disposition. A declared way the army is prepared to seek victory.

Constantia studies the ground.

"Faith that cannot hold anything is easy to admire from a distance."

Aurelia looks at her.

"That sounds almost like criticism of theologians."

"It is criticism of distance."

Eulalia suppresses a smile.

The official heritage of the Sacred Rose enters the room only where it belongs.

Saint Arabella is remembered as level-headed and serene, and as a liberator of the oppressed. Her Order is known for prayers of fortitude and strength rather than cries of bloodlust.

Constantia does not become Arabella.

She does not inherit a saint's private thoughts because the project gives the Silver Compass a Sacred Rose inheritance.

What the resemblance offers is a legitimate tradition through which her own established theology can be understood.

Liberation.

From Latin liberare: to make free.

Constantia's project theology has already pushed the word farther than release from immediate captivity.

A person freed from an enemy but denied any future worth returning to has been rescued incompletely.

Home matters.

Ordinary meals matter.

Children who grow old enough to disagree with their parents matter.

A Sister who survives a battle has possibilities a martyr no longer has.

And yet the Adepta Sororitas do not enter war expecting safety.

That tension sits between the dice.

Constantia places one hand beside them.

"An Act of Faith does not mean the Emperor has relieved us of judgment."

The Paragon pilot nearest the table asks, "Then what does it mean to you?"

Constantia is quiet for a moment.

She does not answer for all Sisters.

"That certainty, when it comes, is also a responsibility."

She points toward the first die.

"If I know this can succeed, I must still ask whether it should."

The second.

"If I can make another thing succeed in the same phase, I must ask what I have denied the next Sister by doing so."

No one speaks over her.

The room is quiet because everyone is listening.

Constantia looks toward the Warlord mark beside her name on the preserved roster.

"That word is smaller than people think."

Warlord.

In the rules, it identifies the selected Character who leads the army.

It does not make every other authority subordinate outside the game.

It does not give Constantia command of Aurelia's ship or Justina's office.

But inside the Sororitas force, the mark is appropriate.

The Order is hers to command.

Her Sisters are not counters she borrowed from you.

Their competence does not begin when you notice them.

Their danger does not become real only when a model is threatened on a table.

Constantia looks at the Retributors in the display.

Then at the Dialogus attached beside them.

Then at her own place in that formation.

"What does a Warlord preserve?" one of the pilots asks.

Constantia does not answer, "herself."

"The mission."

A pause.

"The force capable of completing it."

Another.

"And where possible, the futures of the people carrying it."

The answer does not erase sacrifice.

It gives sacrifice a boundary.

Loss must serve something.

Faith must serve something.

Even certainty must serve something.

Aurelia slowly pushes the two Miracle dice apart again.

One now.

One later.

"Options," she says.

Constantia nods.

Then she pushes them together.

"Concentration."

Apart.

"Endurance."

Together.

"Decision."

Justina watches the movement.

"That is more useful than saying faith makes them reliable."

"It does make them reliable," Eulalia says.

Justina looks toward her.

Eulalia adds, "But reliability is a consequence. Not the whole meaning."

Constantia looks toward you.

She does not tell you what to choose.

The two dice remain before her.

Army of Faith has made the question sharper.

Not whether faith exists.

Not whether the resource can be spent.

The question is what deserves certainty.

And because Constantia is the Warlord, the next lesson belongs naturally to her:

not how to use every miracle,

but how to decide which moment is worthy of one.
